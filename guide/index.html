02 · Equilibrium

Where p meets n, the carriers disappear

p-type silicon has mobile holes (○), n-type has mobile electrons (●). Put them together and both diffuse across the junction and recombine.

They leave behind the dopant atoms, now uncovered ions fixed in the crystal: ⊖ on the p-side, ⊕ on the n-side. This carrier-free zone is the depletion region, and its charge sets up a built-in voltage:

$$V_{bi}=\frac{kT}{q}\ln\frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2}=0.714\ \text{V}$$
Assumptions

Silicon at 300 K, ni = 10¹⁰ cm⁻³, NA = ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, every dopant ionised, uniform doping on each side, one dimension.

03 · The chain ρ → E → V

Charge makes the field, the field makes the voltage

Everything about the junction follows from one chain. Each step is an integral (Gauss's law, then the definition of potential).

  1. Charge ρ(x). −qNA over xp, +qND over xn. The two areas are equal: $N_A x_p = N_D x_n$.
  2. Field E(x). Integrate ρ/ε: a triangle, zero at both edges, largest at the junction, pointing from n to p. $E_{max}=\dfrac{qN_Ax_p}{\varepsilon_s}$
  3. Potential V(x). Integrate E: two parabolas. The area of the triangle is the whole voltage across it: $V_{bi}+V_R=\tfrac12E_{max}W$
Assumptions

Depletion approximation: no free carriers inside −xp … xn, exactly neutral outside, and sharp edges. A full numerical solution softens the edges over a few Debye lengths (≈ 40 nm here) but barely changes W.

04 · Reverse bias

Reverse voltage pulls the junction apart

Connect p to − and n to +. The battery pulls holes and electrons away from the junction, more ions are uncovered, and the depletion region widens. The bigger charge makes a stronger field.

$$W=\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_s}{q}\Big(\frac1{N_A}+\frac1{N_D}\Big)(V_{bi}+V_R)}$$ $$E_{max}=\frac{2(V_{bi}+V_R)}{W}$$

Predict: if (Vbi + VR) becomes 4× bigger, what does W do?

Assumptions

Abrupt junction, depletion approximation, the voltage drops only across the depletion region (no series resistance), and the diode is long enough that W never reaches a contact.

What the ammeter adds up

Ideal (Shockley) diffusion current $I_s=qAn_i^2\big(\tfrac{D_p}{L_pN_D}+\tfrac{D_n}{L_nN_A}\big)\approx1.5$ fA: minority carriers that wander into the junction from the neutral sides. It is constant in reverse bias.

Generation current $I_{gen}=\dfrac{qn_iWA}{2\tau}$: heat creates electron–hole pairs inside the depletion region and the field sweeps them out. It grows with W, so like √(Vbi + VR). The ideal diode equation leaves this out, yet in silicon it is about 1000× larger.

Near breakdown both are multiplied by $M=1/\big(1-(V_R/V_{BR})^4\big)$ (Miller). The supply limits the current to 1 mA, which protects the diode.

Diode: 100 µm × 100 µm, Dn = 35, Dp = 12 cm²/s, all lifetimes 1 µs.

06 · Beyond the step: the graded junction

When the doping changes gradually

Real junctions made by diffusion are not a sharp step. Near the junction the doping changes linearly: $N_D-N_A=a\,x$. Same chain, different shapes. ρ becomes a ramp, E becomes a parabola, V a cubic:

The exponent is the fingerprint: on a log–log plot W against (Vbi+VR) is a straight line of slope ½ or ⅓. A capacitance measurement (C = εA/W) reads it off a real diode.

Assumptions

Gradient a = 10²⁰ cm⁻⁴ (the doping changes by 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ per µm). The depletion region is then symmetric, ±W/2. Built-in voltage: $V_{bi}=\tfrac{2kT}{q}\ln\tfrac{aW_0}{2n_i}$, solved together with W₀ (0.667 V).

07 · Breakdown

The field has a limit

Emax keeps growing with VR. Around Ecrit ≈ 4·10⁵ V/cm an electron crossing the junction gains enough energy to knock a new electron–hole pair loose, which knocks more loose: an avalanche. The current shoots up.

$$V_{BR}=\frac{\varepsilon_sE_{crit}^2}{2qN_{eff}}-V_{bi}\approx103\ \text{V}$$ $$N_{eff}=\frac{N_AN_D}{N_A+N_D}$$

Lighter doping makes the field ramp up more slowly, so it takes more voltage to reach Ecrit. Diodes built to block high voltages are lightly doped.

Assumptions and what we simplify

We take one fixed Ecrit. In reality it rises slowly with doping (≈ 3–6·10⁵ V/cm). Very heavily doped junctions break down earlier by tunnelling (Zener breakdown) instead of avalanche. Breakdown is not damage by itself: the current must be limited, which our lab's supply does.

09 · Recap

The whole story in four lines

  1. Charge sets the field. Uncovered ions in the depletion region make a triangular field, largest at the junction.
  2. Voltage stretches it. Reverse bias uncovers more charge: W ∝ √(Vbi+VR) and Emax = 2(Vbi+VR)/W.
  3. The current stays tiny. Only pA leak, and the leak grows with W. The real diode beats the ideal one.
  4. The field limits it. At Ecrit the avalanche starts: breakdown. A graded junction follows the same chain with W ∝ (Vbi+VR)1/3.
Everything we assumed, in one place
  • Silicon, 300 K, ni = 10¹⁰ cm⁻³, εs = 11.7 ε0; one dimension.
  • Uniform doping on each side (abrupt) or a constant gradient a (graded); every dopant ionised; non-degenerate.
  • Depletion approximation: sharp edges, no free carriers inside, neutral outside.
  • All applied voltage drops across the depletion region; ohmic contacts; W never reaches the contacts.
  • Currents: ideal diffusion current plus generation in the depletion region (τ = 1 µs); no surface leakage; Miller multiplication near breakdown.
  • One constant Ecrit = 4·10⁵ V/cm; no tunnelling (Zener) breakdown; no self-heating.
